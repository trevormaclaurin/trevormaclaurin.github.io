This study documented a facultative feeding commensalism that American Oystercatchers (_Haematopus palliatus_) support for Ruddy Turnstones (_Arenaria interpres_) and Sanderlings (_Calidris alba_). Some breeding American Oystercatchers in Virginia, U.S.A. strip the eggs from mole crabs (_Emerita talpoida_) and leave the carapaces with remaining flesh as discards. We made 83 observations on American Oystercatchers and 179 observations on Ruddy Turnstones and Sanderlings to show that both species follow American Oystercatchers to eat the discarded carapaces, and in doing so increase their mole crab consumption. An American Oystercatcher stripping eggs from mole crabs had an average of 2.28 ± 0.26 (SE) Ruddy Turnstones and 4.16 ± 0.70 Sanderlings following it. The presence of an American Oystercatcher stripping eggs from mole crabs increased mole crab consumption for Ruddy Turnstones by 15 times and Sanderlings by 1.4 times. American Oystercatchers supported a stronger foraging commensalism for Ruddy Turnstones who reduced their foraging effort for obtaining prey themselves (46% reduction). Ruddy Turnstones were more aggressive foragers, and pursued American Oystercatchers stripping eggs from mole crabs, while Sanderlings opportunistically took nearby discards. Mole crab egg stripping by American Oystercatchers provides an easily accessible food source that supplements diets and fuels migrations for Ruddy Turnstones and Sanderlings.
